rF2SkrOverlayPublic is a plugin that draws a driving HUD straight into the rFactor 2 picture: radar, gear and speed, pedal and force-feedback trace, lap delta, relative timing, fuel and tyre data, tyre temperatures, an overhead track map, an FFB frequency graph and a few audio cues. It learns from your own driving (fuel use, pit-lane speed limit, shift points, best laps) and saves it per car and track.

Every panel can be shown or hidden and moved or resized in game, and the layout is remembered separately for each car. The pictures below are drawn diagrams to show the layout and colours; they are not screenshots.

Why it draws inside the game

Most sim-racing overlays (SimHub dashboards, Discord, MSI Afterburner's on-screen display and many HUD apps) are a separate, transparent window laid on top of the game. That costs more than it looks.

Windows can deliver a game's frames to the screen in two ways. With Independent Flip, each finished frame goes straight to the display, and a G-Sync/FreeSync monitor can refresh the moment a frame is ready. With Composed Flip, every frame first goes through the Windows desktop compositor (DWM) so it can be layered with whatever else is on screen. As soon as another window sits over the game, even a fully transparent, click-through one that draws nothing, Windows has to use Composed Flip for every frame for as long as that window is there. That adds latency and takes the direct hand-off away from variable refresh, which is why transparent overlays get blamed for stutter and input lag.

rF2SkrOverlayPublic doesn't use a window at all. It hooks rFactor 2's own DirectX 11 output and draws the HUD into each frame just before the game hands it to Windows, so the game keeps the direct path. It still does a small amount of drawing work every frame; the frame-time stats panel shows what your frames cost.

Tip: cap your frame rate a few FPS below your monitor's refresh rate (rFactor 2's own limiter or RTSS). Frames above the refresh rate are never shown, cause tearing, and push a G-Sync/FreeSync monitor out of its variable-refresh range.

Installing

  1. Close rFactor 2.
  2. Copy rF2SkrOverlayPublic.dll into <Steam>\steamapps\common\rFactor 2\Bin64\Plugins\.
  3. Start rFactor 2. If the HUD does not appear, go to Settings → Gameplay → Plugins, switch rF2SkrOverlayPublic on and restart the game.

A settings file, rF2SkrOverlayPublic.ini, is created next to the DLL on first run.

Nothing shows in the main menus: the plugin starts drawing once you load into a session (the garage), so it never touches the game's graphics while it is still starting up and setting the screen mode.

Requires 64-bit rFactor 2 running DirectX 11, and the Microsoft Visual C++ 2015–2022 x64 runtime (most sim PCs already have it). It does not work in Le Mans Ultimate: LMU runs plugins in a separate process, so they cannot draw into the game picture.

Updating

Close rFactor 2 and replace the DLL. Your rF2SkrOverlayPublic.ini and everything the plugin has learned are kept. If you still have an earlier release, rF2SkrOverlay.dll or rF2RadarOverlay.dll, delete it, or both will run and draw on top of each other. This release starts with fresh settings in its own rF2SkrOverlayPublic.ini; the learned fuel, pit-limit, shift-point and best-lap data is shared with the earlier releases and carries over.

Version numbers

The version is the date it was built, then a build count: year.month.day.build. For example, 26.11.11.2 is the second build made on 11 November 2026. A higher number is always a newer version. The version is shown at the top of this guide and near the bottom of the in-game shortcut list.

Uninstalling

Close rFactor 2 and delete rF2SkrOverlayPublic.dll and rF2SkrOverlayPublic.ini from Bin64\Plugins. The learned data folders listed under Settings & files can be deleted too.

Rotary menus (keyboard or wheel)

Three small menus let you change things while driving without the mouse. Each has an "up" and a "down" key, and you can also put them on rotary encoders or buttons on your wheel.

While you use one, a 10-line list appears at the side of the screen: the left knob's on the left, the right knob's and the on/off list on the right. Each list keeps the same width whatever is highlighted, with its entries numbered down the left and the highlighted setting's value on the right. The highlight moves as you turn or press; when it steps onto the last line at the top or bottom, the list slides 5 lines so the highlight is back in the middle. The box at the bottom shows both knobs' settings, L on the left and R on the right, also at a fixed width (long values are shortened). A line above each list names the wheel button that acts on it (switch on/off, or change the value). The settings come in coloured groups, in this order: brakes and chassis (ABS, brake bias, front and rear anti-roll bar), engine and TC (traction control, TC power cut, TC slip, engine mixture, shift point), strategy (fuel stint length, tyre max wear, tyre target time), HUD (pit lane limit, track map colour), cover boxes (opacity, shade), driving position (field of view, seat fore/aft, seat height) and headlights. A setting whose panel is switched off is greyed out with "(panel off)" and the knob steps over it. Each setting has a small icon beside it. Brake bias shows the car's real front share from telemetry; the others that press rF2 keys count the steps sent. The on/off list starts with an ESC entry: switching it presses Esc in rFactor 2, to close the chat box (which otherwise takes the keys as typed text) or go back to the garage. "Up" (or turning counter-clockwise) moves the highlight up.

Radar and side bars

A top-down radar around your car. Other cars are drawn at their class's real size and orientation: GT3, GT4, GTE, TCR, prototypes, single-seaters, NASCAR, IndyCar, trucks and more. A car close enough to matter turns red.

Side proximity bars run down the left and right edges of the screen while a car is alongside you, for your peripheral vision. They are triggered by exactly the same rule that turns a radar blip red. How close the car is shows in the bar's strength. Its shape leans towards the end of your car the other car is at. Two cars alongside on one side read stronger than one.

An optional safety margin around every car can be set with [Radar] FootprintBufferMeters.

Gear and speed

Gear and speed on a dark panel, with a small box to the left of the gear digit that only appears when it has something to say.

Gear digit

  • Red flash when the revs are near the shift point.
  • Blue flash is the "shift now" cue. You choose the RPM to shift at with the Shift point rotary setting (1% of max RPM a click, saved for each car). The cue fires early by your measured reaction time, and for each gear it learns how much earlier still, from your full-throttle upshifts made in response to it, so the actual shift lands on your target. It does not show in top gear.
  • Red background when you sit in neutral for more than 0.2 s. A quick pass through neutral on an upshift doesn't trigger it. There is also an optional first-gear warning.

Speed box

Tints amber while a wheel locks under braking and cyan while a wheel spins under power.

The small box (left of the gear)

  • PIT flashes while the pit limiter is on: green at or under the pit-lane limit. Over it, the box turns red, reads OVER SPEED and flashes twice as fast. Gear and speed stay on screen as normal. The limit is the one you set for the track (the Pit lane limit knob setting, see Warnings above the dashboard); without one, it is learned the first time you drive with the limiter on, saved per car and track, and the box stays amber until then. A learned limit is only where your limiter holds the car, so set the real one if the car's limiter allows more than the pit lane does.
  • ABS flashes amber and TC flashes cyan while the car's ABS or traction control is acting. These are the same colours as the brake and throttle in the pedal trace. ABS is detected from the brake pressure at each wheel. Each can be switched off in the shortcut list.

Shift stats, a separate small panel, shows how you actually shift: average and earliest shift RPM, per gear, and your reaction time to the cue. It keeps its width as you shift, so it does not jump about at every gear change.

Tyre lockup box

A 2×2 box next to the speed readout, one square per wheel in the car's real layout. Each square lights up while that wheel locks under braking: the more it locks, the brighter it gets.

Warnings above the dashboard

Yellow lines just above the gear and speed boxes, shown only while there is something to warn about.

Stopped cars ahead

  • STOPPED 420 m for a car under 40 km/h: on a racing track, as good as stopped. In a corner you take that slowly yourself (a hairpin), a car only counts under 80% of your own best-lap speed there, so normal traffic in the hairpin is not reported.
  • Stopped cars within 150 m of each other are one incident: STOPPED 420 m (3 cars), the distance to the first of them.
  • OFF TRACK is added when the car (or every car in the pile-up) is beyond the track edge rather than on the racing surface. Without it, at least one is on the track.
  • The distance counts down as you close in, and the line flashes inside 200 m.
  • Only the nearest incident within 1000 m ahead is shown. A car must stay slow for a second before it counts, so one that only runs wide is not reported. Cars in the pit lane, cars that have taken the flag, the first 10 s after the green flag and full-course yellows are left out.

Yellow flags

YELLOW S2 names each sector with a local yellow flag. This needs the server to run local yellows; with them off, only the slow-car warning works. During a full-course yellow the line reads FULL COURSE YELLOW.

Pit lane limit

Once you request a pit stop, PIT LIMIT 60 km/h appears just above the speed box, until you are in the pit lane or switch the limiter on. Set the limit for each track with the Pit lane limit knob setting (see Rotary menus): one click is 1 km/h, the first click starts from the learned limit, and turning below 20 switches it back off. Without a set limit the line shows the learned one with a "~" in front.

Distances, ranges and the colour are in the [TrackAlerts] section of the INI.

Pedal trace

A scrolling history of the last few seconds:

  • Throttle and brake. A bright line shows what you pressed. A darker line underneath shows what the car actually used after ABS or traction control.
  • Force feedback, centred on the middle line. Up means the wheel is being pushed right and down means left. The top and bottom edges are 100%, so a line pinned flat against an edge is the force feedback clipping.
  • Marks where a wheel locked or spun.

Lap delta and speed strip

The delta bar shows how far ahead or behind you are, compared at the same point on track, against two references:

  • Session best: your fastest lap this session.
  • Best: your all-time best for this car and track, saved across restarts.

Each row can be shown or hidden on its own.

The speed strip above it shows two strips, each one whole lap from left to right: your last completed lap on top and the lap you are on below, filling in as you drive. Each small slice of track is green if you carried more speed through it than the reference lap, and red if less. The delta bar tells you how much time you have gained; the strip tells you where.

A small DRS sign appears under the bar when DRS becomes legal and while it is open.

Relative timing

In a race: the leader plus the cars just ahead of and behind you in the race order. How many are shown is set with the slider in the shortcut list. In practice and qualifying: the cars physically closest to you on track.

Gap box: a second, smaller table with just the two cars ahead of you in the race order, you, and the two behind, picked from the whole field (retired cars left out). It starts just under the standings, moves and resizes on its own in layout mode, and has its own row in the shortcut list.

Riding along: when a team-mate is driving your shared car, rFactor 2 marks no car as yours. The HUD then uses the car you last drove (saved as [Session] LastVehicle), so the standings, gear, speed and fuel follow your car rather than a random one. Use Ctrl+Shift+A to show the HUD while you are not driving.

The footer shows the session's fastest lap and who set it, in purple, followed by your own best lap this session (PB). At the chequered flag the table freezes as it stood, because the game's own gap figures stop making sense once the leader has finished.

Fuel, tyres and brake bias

Three boxes, each of which can be shown or hidden on its own.

Fuel

Fuel left in time and laps. In races it also shows fuel needed to finish and how much over or short you are. Consumption is learned per car class and track from your clean laps; pit laps, refuels and outliers are ignored, and the session's fuel multiplier is allowed for. In practice and qualifying it shows how long the current stint has run.

Tyres

Wear in % per lap for each tyre, under one header. Below that is the time left until your most worn tyre reaches 20% life, naming which tyre it is. In races there is also a projection of tyre life at the finish.

Brake bias

The current brake bias as the front share (56.4% means 56.4:43.6, as in the garage), and how far you have moved it since getting in the car.

Stint targets: fuel saving and tyre wear

For long races run in fixed-length stints. Both are set in the INI, move and resize in layout mode, and can be switched off with their rows in the shortcut list.

Fuel-saving bar

Set the stint length you need the tank to last, and the bar tells you live through every lap whether you are using more or less fuel than that allows. The target litres per lap is worked out from the fuel in the tank, the stint time left and your last lap time: as soon as you leave the pit lane, and again at the start of each lap.

  • The bar fills green while you are under the target for the distance covered so far, and red while you are over it.
  • The table under it shows: Stint target (the stint length you set, and in laps), Stint left (time and laps still to drive in it), Fuel lasts (how long the fuel in the tank lasts at last lap's use, green if that covers the stint left, red if not) and Fuel per lap (your average, and how far last lap was under or over the target).
  • Off until you set a stint length: [FuelSave] StintMinutes=50 (for example). ReserveLitres is how much should be left at the end (default 0.5), and BarWindowLitres how big a difference fills half the bar (default 0.10).

Tyre-wear target

Set how worn a set of tyres may get, and how long it must last. One small box per tyre shows last lap's wear (green when under what that lap needed, red when over) and how much wear per lap each tyre can now afford. Each box is also tinted live through the lap. The clock starts again whenever fresh tyres go on.

  • [TyreSave] TargetWearPercent: the most wear allowed, default 70%.
  • [TyreSave] TargetMinutes: how long the set must last, default 100. Set it to 0 to hide the panel.

Engine temperatures and FFB strength

Water and oil temperatures

rFactor 2 gives plugins the two temperatures and a flag for when the engine is already overheating, but not the car's limits. So the box learns what is normal for this car from your own driving at speed this session: white is normal, amber is well above normal, and red means the game says the engine is overheating. How far above normal turns amber is [EngineTemps] WaterAmberMargin (default 6 °C) and OilAmberMargin (default 8 °C). Some cars report no temperatures to plugins; then the box just says "Engine temp OK", or "OVERHEATING" on red when the game says so.

FFB strength

The force rFactor 2 is sending to your wheel right now, as a percentage of full strength (held at its peak briefly so you can read it), the highest so far this lap, and last lap's highest along with how much of that lap was at 100% (clipping). If the clipping figure is high, turn the car's FFB strength down.

Both boxes move and resize in layout mode. Turn either off with its row in the shortcut list.

Track conditions and weather

A box for mixed-weather races. At the top it gives a tyre call:

  • SLICKS: the racing line is dry enough.
  • SLICKS - near crossover, or wets soon if it is getting wetter: close to the point where wets become faster.
  • WETS: the racing line is wet enough for wets.

The switch-over point is set by [Conditions] WetsAtWetness (default 20% average wetness of the racing line). Below the call it shows how wet the racing line is (average, plus its driest and wettest parts), how hard it is raining, and track and air temperature, each with which way it is heading (wetter or drying, heavier or easing, and how much the track temperature has changed in the last 10 minutes).

Forecasts

  • GAME forecast: rFactor 2's own forecast for this session (rain chance, temperature and sky at five points through the session), read from the game itself on your PC.
  • REAL forecast: the real-world hourly forecast (rain chance, rain amount and temperature) for the real circuit, from the free Open-Meteo service. To see the same data the HUD reads (Spa shown; swap in your circuit's coordinates): api.open-meteo.com/v1/forecast?latitude=50.633&longitude=5.567&…. This is the only part of the plugin that uses the internet, and all it sends is the circuit's map coordinates. Well-known circuits are built in. For any other track, add CoordsLat_<track name>= and CoordsLon_<track name>= under [Conditions]. It is off by default: switch it on with the Real weather forecast row in the shortcut list or [Conditions] RealWeather=1.

The track map can also show which parts of the lap were wet when you last drove them: see Track map. The box moves and resizes in layout mode, and the Track conditions row in the shortcut list turns it off.

Tyre temperatures

After each corner, three bars per tyre show the peak surface temperature across the tread (outside, middle, inside), laid out like the car seen from above. All four tyres share one scale, so they compare directly. The hottest zone on any tyre is full height.

Colour shows the temperature itself: cold, in the window and hot. The tyres that carried the load in that corner are drawn brighter. It is meant for judging camber: inside a little hotter than outside is the usual target.

Track map

An overhead map of the circuit with a dot for every car and a label for each, your own car highlighted. The map is recorded from your own first clean lap at a new track and saved, so it is there straight away next time. If TinyPedal is installed and has already recorded the track, its map is used instead ([TrackMap] TinyPedalMapDir). The box fits the shape of the track, and a small N arrow on a small disc just above the box's top-right corner shows north (outside the box, so the track never runs through it).

Colouring by mini-sector

The lap is split into equal mini-sectors (80 by default, set with a slider in the shortcut list), and each one can be coloured against a reference:

  • green where you were faster and red where you were slower;
  • purple where you matched or beat the fastest time anyone in your class has set through that mini-sector this session.

The reference is off, your personal best, your session best or the session's fastest lap by anyone in your class. Click the row in the shortcut list to change it, or use wheel buttons 30 and 31 (set in [TrackMap] as DeltaPrevButton/DeltaNextButton).

Rotation, mirror and re-record

Each circuit's map can be turned to any angle, mirrored, or thrown away and recorded again on the next clean lap, from the shortcut list or the controls under the map in layout mode. The setting is kept for that circuit.

FFB spectrum

Shows which frequencies the force feedback rFactor 2 sends to your wheel contains, live, from 1 Hz up to 200 Hz (half the game's 400 Hz FFB rate, the most that signal can carry). The frequency axis is a log scale, labelled at the same points as Moza Pit House's FFB equaliser (5, 10, 15, 25, 30, 40, 50, 60, 80, 100 Hz, plus 150 and 200), so you can read it straight across to your EQ.

The line is drawn in log bands, 5 per decade by default (about 1, 1.6, 2.5, 4, 6.3, 10, 16, 25, 40, 63, 100, 160 and 200 Hz). Each point is the combined energy of everything in its band, so the high bands, which cover more frequencies, sit a little higher than the low ones. [FfbGraph] BandsPerDecade=10 gives finer 1/3-octave bands; 0 draws one point per 1 Hz instead. The live line covers about the last second of driving and updates up to 60 times a second; Realtime=0 averages it over WindowSeconds instead.

  • 1–5 Hz: the steering weight building and easing through corners (the steady load itself is shown by the FFB strength box).
  • 5–25 Hz: kerbs, bumps and the car moving around.
  • 25 Hz and up: road texture, vibration and grain.

The vertical axis is a log scale in % of full force, so small details are still visible next to the big steering forces.

Clipping % (top right) is how much of the last 10 s the force spent at 100%. While it is clipping, changes in force are cut off, so you feel nothing until it drops back below 100%. Short clips on kerbs are harmless. Steady clipping through fast corners means the car's FFB setting is too high for that car.

The red line is the spectrum at the moment it last clipped, fading out over 2 s. It shows what pushed it over the limit. Red highest at the low end means the corner load itself; red in the middle means kerbs or bumps.

The title shows the signal's own rate (normally 400 Hz). This graph reads what the game sends; your wheel base's own filters and EQ act on it after that.

Cover boxes

Plain rounded boxes you can place anywhere, to hide part of the car's own dashboard or in-car display that competes with the overlay. Up to four, each moved and resized (width and height separately, from any corner) in layout mode.

All the boxes share one shade, from black to white, and one opacity, both set with sliders in the shortcut list. They are drawn underneath every other panel. Off by default: switch on Cover boxes in the shortcut list, then place them in layout mode.

Sounds and cues

CueWhat it does
DRSA double beep when DRS becomes legal to use, plus an "OPEN DRS" or "DRS ENGAGED" sign under the delta bar.
Have your audio device switched on before starting rFactor 2. The game itself can crash while loading a car if it starts with no sound device.

Braking-point beep

Beeps just before each place where you started braking on your best lap of the session, as a reminder of where your braking point was. It is off by default: switch on the Brake cue + beep row in the shortcut list (or set ShowBrakeCue=1 in [Shortcuts]).

  • It needs one complete, valid lap first. When you set a faster lap, its braking points replace the old ones.
  • The beep comes 0.3 s before the braking point ([BrakeCue] LeadTimeSeconds), so that with a normal reaction your foot reaches the pedal at about the right place.
  • Only big stops beep. The Brake beep slider in the shortcut list sets how much speed a stop must lose on your best lap to get a beep, from every braking point up to 150 km/h. The default is 50 km/h.
  • A small Brake React box near the bottom of the screen shows how long you took to touch the brake after each beep (the average and the last one). It moves and resizes in layout mode like the other panels, and can be hidden with [BrakeCue] ShowReactionStats=0.
  • The pitch and length of the beep are BeepFrequencyHz and BeepDurationMs in [BrakeCue].
The beep follows your own best lap, not an ideal one. Treat it as a reminder, not a target: if you find you can brake later, do, and the next best lap moves the beeps with you.

Frame-time stats and clock

A fixed-size box with FPS and frame time. More stats can be switched on in the [FrameLatency] section of the INI: CPU and GPU time per frame, FPS range with 1% low, game RAM, VRAM, and GPU power (AMD cards). This box also stays up out of the car (garage, monitor, and the menus after your first session) as a sign the plugin is running. A small clock can be shown as well.

The RAM stat (ShowPcRam) reads RAM 18.2 used, rF2 9.1, 13.8 free GB: the whole PC's memory in use, the part rFactor 2 uses, and what is free. The VRAM stat (ShowVram) reads VRAM 9.8 used, rF2 8.1, 6.2 free GB: the graphics card's memory in use by everything (the same figure RTSS and Afterburner show), the part rFactor 2 uses, and what is free on the card.

Replay recording (ShowReplay=1, off by default) adds how rFactor 2 is recording the replay: Replay off, Replay RAM, or Replay disk 1.24 GB with the size of the recording so far, followed by the replay fidelity setting, e.g. High fidelity (Low, Medium, High or Max). The size turns amber from 1.5 GB and red from 1.9 GB: a recording that reached 2 GB was seen to stop and never be saved. A replay kept in RAM cannot be measured on its own; it is part of rF2 RAM.

DLL cost (by module), off by default, shows how much time the plugin itself takes each frame, split by panel, as a bar per module. Switch it on in the shortcut list if you want to see what the HUD costs, or to find out which panel to turn off on a slow PC. It moves and resizes in layout mode.

Settings and files

Everything the shortcut list and layout mode change is saved automatically, once you are out of the car (back in the garage or at the monitor). What the plugin learns while you drive (best lap, braking points, fuel and tyre use, pit limit, shift points, track maps) is saved as you cross the line. Nothing is written to disk at any other moment, so saving never costs you a frame. If the game crashes mid-stint, only that lap's learning and the layout or setting changes made since you got in are lost. Everything else (colours, thresholds, which stats show, hotkeys) is in Bin64\Plugins\rF2SkrOverlayPublic.ini. Edit it with the game closed; new values take effect on the next start.

Update check

Each time the game starts, the plugin asks GitHub once which release is the latest. If a newer one is out, a message says so (once, for 15 seconds) the first time you are in the garage, never while driving, with the address of the releases page. Nothing is downloaded or installed: you get the new version yourself. Nothing about you is sent either; GitHub sees the request like any website visit. If there is no internet connection, nothing shows. To switch it off, turn off Update check in the on/off list (shortcut list or centre rotary), or set CheckForUpdates=0 under [Updates] in the INI.

Layouts per car

Which panels are shown, where they sit and how big they are is remembered separately for each car model. The first time you drive a new car it starts from your current layout; after that, getting into a car brings back the layout you last used in it.

The log is Bin64\rF2SkrOverlayPublic.log. The previous run's log is kept as rF2SkrOverlayPublic.prev.log. Send these if you report a problem.

Text font

Two rows near the bottom of the shortcut list change the font used for all the overlay's text. Click Text font to cycle through Auto, Consolas, DejaVu Sans Mono, Lucida Console and Courier New, and Text weight to cycle through Light, Normal and Bold. The change shows straight away and is saved.

Auto uses Windows' Consolas font, or DejaVu Sans Mono, which is built into the plugin, when Consolas isn't installed. That is the case when running the game on Linux through Steam (Proton), where Consolas, Lucida Console and Courier New are usually missing too, so picking them shows DejaVu Sans Mono instead. If the text looks thin or hard to read, try Normal or Bold.

Optional diagnostic logging

Off by default. Turn these on in the INI only if asked to, because they make the log much larger:

  • [RelativeTiming] LogRaceGaps=1 — race gaps every 10 s.
  • [LapDelta] ShowValidityDebug=1 — a small on-screen line under the delta bar showing whether rFactor 2 is counting the current lap (useful if the delta bar never shows a reference).

Changes in this version

Changed

  • Rotary setting lists grouped by colour (brakes and chassis, engine and TC, strategy, HUD, cover boxes, driving position, headlights), with new Traction control and Brake bias settings, greyed-out rows for switched-off panels, and a line naming the wheel button above each list.
  • Rotary lists, knob box and messages are drawn above every other panel, text included.
  • Tyre wear target boxes are narrower, without the empty space inside.
  • Fuel-saving bar: a clearer table underneath, and the target is set as soon as you leave the pit lane.
  • Track map: the north arrow sits outside the box; the wet sections colour mode is removed (it did not show the wet parts reliably).
  • Shift cue: you set the shift point (new rotary setting), the cue learns its timing per gear, saved per car entry. No shift cue in top gear; the shift stats box no longer jumps at gear changes.
  • Icons beside each rotary setting.

New

  • Update check: at each game start the plugin asks GitHub whether a newer release is out and says so in a message in the garage. Nothing is downloaded. Off with Update check in the on/off list. See Settings and files.
  • Rotary menus: on/off list and two setting lists, driven by Home/End, Insert/Delete and Page Up/Page Down, or your wheel's buttons. See Rotary menus.
  • Gap box: the two cars ahead and two behind you in the race, in their own small table. See Relative timing.
  • Your session best (PB) in the standings footer, after the session's fastest lap.
  • HUD always on (Ctrl+Shift+A): shows the HUD while you are not driving, such as riding along in a shared car.
  • Track conditions and weather box: slicks/wets call, wetness and rain trends, the game's forecast and an optional real-world forecast. See Track conditions.
  • DLL cost panel (off by default): what each panel costs per frame.
  • Shortcut-list rows to switch the fuel save bar, tyre wear target, water/oil temps, FFB strength, track conditions and real weather on or off.
  • Replay recording in the frame-time stats box: off, RAM or disk with its size so far, and the replay fidelity. See Frame-time stats.
  • Stopped car warning above the dashboard, with a distance countdown, a car count for a pile-up and OFF TRACK (no sound). See Warnings above the dashboard.
  • Yellow flag sector (YELLOW S2) and full-course yellow in the same line.
  • Pit lane limit per track, set with a knob setting. The PIT box reads OVER SPEED when you are above it with the limiter on, and PIT LIMIT 60 km/h shows above the speed box once you request a stop.

Changed

  • Pit limiter: the big PIT bar is gone. PIT now flashes in the small ABS/TC box (green under the limit, red over it), and gear and speed stay visible.
  • FFB spectrum: now goes up to 200 Hz, on a log frequency axis labelled at the Moza EQ points, drawn in log bands (BandsPerDecade). See FFB spectrum.
  • Layout mode: every panel, cover boxes included, resizes from any of its four corners, and the opposite corner stays put instead of the panel growing out of its centre. The standings and gap box show at their full size while you place them.
  • Brake bias is shown as the front share, the way the garage and car dashes give it.
  • Shortcut list: every panel and option is now in one grouped list (Driving, Timing, Fuel/tyres/brakes, Car & FFB, Track & weather, Screen), and while it is open every switched-on panel is shown at full size.
  • Pedal trace uses less CPU (flat stretches of a pedal line are drawn as one piece), and its lines no longer change shape as they scroll.
  • Tyre wear plots removed.
  • Shortcut list is more compact (smaller text, tighter rows) on a darker background.
  • Riding along in a shared car: the HUD follows your own car instead of mixing in other cars' data (which made gear, speed and the PIT warning jump around).
  • Stint helpers: laps-based stint info, tyre life per corner and the stint clock counting only time on track.
  • The track map is kept on the graphics card and only rebuilt when it changes, which cuts its CPU cost a lot on long tracks.
  • When files are saved: only when you cross the line (newly learned data) or are out of the car (everything else), on a background thread. See Settings and files.
  • Pit stints are no longer saved across a game restart.
  • Smoother frame times: no more hitches when leaving layout mode, at a beep, at a car or track change, or when the track map's sector colours change. Each frame also costs less to draw.
  • The track conditions and engine temps boxes update five times a second.
  • GPU usage in the stats line counts only rFactor 2's own GPU work.
  • Pit-limiter reminder removed (the "ENGAGE PIT LIMITER" warning and its beep). The PitEntryOffsets folder is no longer used and can be deleted.

Fixed

  • Wheel buttons no longer stay dead when the wheel was not found at game start: the plugin looks again every 2 s.
  • Two possible crashes: one when the game recreates its window, one when a setting changes while saving.
  • The frame-time stats box could overrun its memory with every extra stat switched on.
  • Changing car no longer keeps the previous car's per-gear shift points.
